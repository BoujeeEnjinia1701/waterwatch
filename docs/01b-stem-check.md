# WaterWatch STEM check

Verdict: the working principle of option A, an hourly timed flush through a small dark flow cell read by an 860 nm nephelometer and a membrane-free amperometric chlorine sensor, powered by a small panel and one LiFePO4 cell, sits within known science. One detail failed at the first check: the battery was specified as "1S2P 32700 cells, 3.2 V, 6 Ah", but one 32700 LiFePO4 cell already holds 6 Ah, so the stated capacity, the cell count and the thermal mass in the calculation disagreed. It was fixed under Amish's standing instruction of 2026-10-23 by specifying one 32700 cell (WWT-DEC-001 F1). The independent review then weakened 17 rows and found 7 missing claims; four more fixes were made under the same instruction (F5 to F8: a longer flush at low-pressure sites, a valve rated for 4 bar, TLS session resumption and a second check valve at the cell inlet), and its corrected chlorine error budget gave one row that failed with a fix that changes a requirement, so it went to Amish (S31; WWT-DEC-001 O5). Amish decided on 2026-10-25 ("i concur with your recommendations") to restate PR-001 as ±30 % above 1.0 mg/L (O5) and to make a shared DPD photometer the monthly reference (O2); with both, the budget meets all 8 cases and S31 is within known science (WWT-CAL-001 E11, E12). Of the 37 claims of option A, **28 are within known science and 9 are plausible, to confirm by test at TRL 4**. The plausible rows are the hot-day recharge (S4) and the sun shield factor it depends on (S5), the chlorine sensor's drift (S12), its pseudo-reference (S14), its flow dependence (S32) and low-conductivity water (S36), fouling (S22), freezing (S33) and the sun-heating of the temperature reading (S34). None can be closed by calculation, so `stem_check` is **open**. Options B and C have 6 claims each; each has 2 plausible rows and none fails. Method: STANDARDS section 20. Calculations: `docs/04-calcs/stem_check.py`, which imports the calculation note's script `docs/04-calcs/sizing.py` (WWT-CAL-001 v0.6); the table is written by `docs/04-calcs/stm_table.py`.

## Concept options

- **Option A, timed-flush flow cell on its own pole:** the S rows above. Within known science; open on S5, S12, S14 and S22.
- **Option B, reagent colorimetric analyser cabinet:** within known science in principle (DPD colorimetry is the reference method, B1); reagent life in a hot cabinet (B2) and the discharge of reacted sample (B5) are plausible, to assess.
- **Option C, in-riser probe manifold:** within known science as plumbing and electrochemistry; ORP as a free chlorine estimate (C1) is plausible but unlikely to meet PR-001, and bubble bias in running water (C4) is to assess. Cutting the riser conflicts with the problem statement's constraint of one tee (C6), a scope point, not a science failure.

## Independent review

The independent reviewer was a separate Claude session, started on 2026-10-25 with read access only to this document, the calculation note WWT-CAL-001, the product requirements WWT-PRS-001 and the sizing script with its printed output, and none of the author's reasoning.
