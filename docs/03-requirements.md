# WaterWatch product requirements and specifications

What the design must do, each requirement traced to a user need in WWT-URS-001. This document replaces the requirements WWT-REQ-001 (v0.6): every R number is carried to a PR number with the same target, verification and TRL 3 status, and the old number is in the "Was" column. The targets are proposals for review with a co-design partner, not yet validated needs. Status comes from the calculation note WWT-CAL-001 v0.7. On paper, of the 16 requirements, 3 are at risk (PR-002 turbidity, PR-009 outdoor survival and PR-010 caretaker maintenance), and PR-012 cost is over the value-engineering target by USD 25. PR-001 free chlorine was not met at 1.5 mg/L after the independent review of the STEM check corrected the error budget; Amish decided on 2026-10-25 ("i concur with your recommendations") to restate it as ±30 % above 1.0 mg/L (WWT-DEC-001 O5) and to use a shared DPD photometer as the monthly reference (O2), so it is now met on paper (WWT-CAL-001 E11, E12). On paper 12 of the 16 are met (6 by calculation and 6 by design). Method: STANDARDS section 22. The traceability matrix (WWT-RTM-001) is generated by `python .kit/trace.py`.

Notes on status:

- **PR-001.** The budget of WWT-CAL-001 v0.5 met the target in all 8 cases at 25 °C before drift (E6). With the stale-water bias added linearly, as the independent review asked, 6 of 8 cases are met; at 1.5 mg/L it misses by 0.004 mg/L at pH 7.5 and 0.017 mg/L at pH 8.5 (E9), and when the sensor is calibrated at 0.5 mg/L and reads 1.5 mg/L only 1 of 4 high cases is met (E10). At 0.2 to 0.5 mg/L, where the alerts are set, it was met with at least 0.13 mg/L left for drift. Amish restated the target as ±30 % above 1.0 mg/L on 2026-10-25 (WWT-DEC-001 O5), which the comparator budget meets in all 8 cases (E11); with the shared DPD photometer (±0.03 mg/L ±3 %, WWT-MKT-001) as the monthly reference (O2) all 8 are met, calibrated at the reading level or at 0.5 mg/L, with at least 0.15 mg/L left for drift (E12). Status not met to met on paper. The drift of the low-cost sensor over a month is unknown (WWT-STM-001 S12, S14).
- **PR-005.** 13.0 min from a confirmed crossing to the SMS with three tries; from the event itself up to 92 min with all tries (WWT-CAL-001 H4).
- **PR-006.** 0.36 MB a month with TLS session resumption (WWT-DEC-001 F7); a full handshake every session would use 1.22 MB (G3).
- **PR-008.** 19.8 L/day at the regulator's tolerance on the hourly schedule; at a 0.5 bar site with the 110 s flush (F5) 15.4 L/day; confirming readings add about 0.25 L/day; Amish decided on 2026-10-25 that PR-008 counts the hourly schedule only, as the requirement now says (WWT-DEC-001 O4).
- **PR-014.** Two check valves in series (supply train and cell inlet, WWT-DEC-001 F8) and the normally closed valve stand between the cell and the supply; a verifiable backflow device or an inlet air gap is to assess under the local code (WWT-REG-001 R4).
- **PR-009.** With the sun shield the inside peaks at 50.8 °C on a 45 °C day, and charging is allowed for 5.4 sun hours (WWT-CAL-001 C4); the shield factor is assumed. The one-cell battery (WWT-DEC-001 F1) lowers the thermal mass from 1,460 to 1,305 J/K and leaves the peaks unchanged to 0.1 °C.
- **PR-012.** USD 325 per base unit, USD 25 over the target (WWT-CAL-001 K1); USD 385 to 425 at a pH probe variant site (K2); the second check valve (WWT-DEC-001 F8) added USD 3.

## Assumptions

- Site: public tapstand on a piped, chlorinated rural scheme, with a supply pressure of 0.5 to 4 bar (7 to 58 psi) at the riser. Tapstands come first; tank outlets and kiosks later (WWT-DDR-001, D8).
- Alert thresholds follow the field guidance summarized in WWT-PRB-001: at least 0.2 mg/L free chlorine at the point of delivery, and chlorination losing effectiveness as turbidity rises. The defaults of 0.2 mg/L and 5 NTU were decided by Amish on 2026-09-25 (WWT-DDR-001, D6) and remain adjustable per site.
- The caretaker's DPD test is the field reference for free chlorine. The v0.3 target of ±0.1 mg/L matched the resolution of common DPD comparator kits, which WWT-CAL-001 showed made it as tight as the reference itself (the comparator alone takes ±0.07 mg/L); R1 was relaxed to ±0.2 mg/L or ±25 % for that reason (WWT-DDR-002).
- pH affects the split between hypochlorous acid and hypochlorite, and amperometric sensors respond mainly to hypochlorous acid. R1 is assessed with a site pH entered at installation and checked monthly (WWT-DDR-001, D2), assumed to move no more than ±0.2 between visits. Under WWT-DDR-002 the pH probe variant (read to ±0.1) is fitted at sites whose pH is above 7.5 or moves more than 0.2 between visits; the cell lid carries a plugged spare port for it.
- Budget: the $300 in `project.yaml` is a value-engineering target for one base unit's parts, not a spending limit (Amish, 2026-10-01). A shared calibration kit (about $60), airtime (about $1 to $3 per month) and, at variant sites only, the pH probe and interface (about $60 to $100) are listed separately; all are estimates.
- Installation: the pole footing is dug and cast on the survey visit, so the pole is set and cured before installation day (WWT-DDR-002).

> **Safety:** Meeting these requirements does not make the water safe to drink or the unit safe to install. The sample line connects to a pressurized drinking water supply and the unit contains a lithium iron phosphate battery; see WWT-PRC-001, Safety.
